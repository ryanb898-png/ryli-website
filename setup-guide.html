Setup Guide

Your camera & overlay, set up right.

Never used OBS before? Start here. This guide covers four things, in order: getting OBS running, adding a phone camera as your product cam with DroidCam, setting up RYLI's overlay on top, and starting your show the right way on Whatnot. Follow it top to bottom your first time — about 15–25 minutes, no experience required.

  • A Windows PC — or a Mac running Parallels Desktop
  • A smartphone with a camera (Android or iPhone)
  • Same Wi-Fi for phone + PC, or a USB cable (more reliable — recommended)
  • About 15–25 minutes, start to finish
1. Install OBS 2. Add DroidCam 3. How it layers 4. Install RYLI 5. Start your show
🎥Step 1 of 5 — Install OBS Studio

Get the free broadcast software running

OBS (Open Broadcaster Software) is the free program that actually sends your stream to Whatnot. Everything else in this guide — your phone camera, RYLI's overlay — plugs into OBS. If you don't have it yet, get it first.

Download OBS Studio
Go to obsproject.com/download and grab the Windows installer — it's free, official, and takes under a minute.
Run the installer
Open the downloaded file and click through with the default options — there's nothing to configure yet. Leave "Launch OBS Studio" checked when it finishes.
Skip the Auto-Configuration Wizard
OBS may offer to auto-configure your stream on first launch. Click "Cancel" — RYLI's own onboarding handles the settings that matter later in this guide.
Confirm OBS opens to an empty scene
You'll see a blank black canvas with empty "Scene" and "Sources" panels. That's normal — you haven't added any cameras yet. That's Step 2.
📱Step 2 of 5 — Add DroidCam

Turn your phone into your product cam

DroidCam turns your phone into a wireless camera OBS can use — most live sellers point it forward at the table so viewers see the product, not just a face. It has two halves — an app on your phone, and a plugin on this PC — you need both.

Install DroidCam on your phone
Get it from Google Play or the Apple App Store. Open it once so it can ask for camera permission.
Install the DroidCam client on this PC
Download it from droidcam.app/windows and run it with the default options. This is the program that receives your phone's video on this computer.
Install the DroidCam OBS plugin
This is a separate small installer from droidcam.app/obs that adds DroidCam as an option inside OBS's own source list. Without it, OBS can't see your phone. Run it, then fully close and reopen OBS.
Connect your phone to the PC client
Open the PC client and the phone app together. Both on the same Wi-Fi works, but a USB cable gives the most stable, lowest-lag feed — that's what we recommend for live selling.
Add DroidCam as an OBS source, pointed at your product
In OBS, click the + under Sources → DroidCam Source → select your phone. Prop it facing forward at your product or table. This feed is your show's main camera — your product cam — for the rest of this guide.
🗂️Step 3 of 5 — How it all layers

Two things in OBS — RYLI does double duty

Any OBS livestream setup already has its own camera — that's DroidCam, from Step 2. RYLI only ever adds ONE more thing to OBS on top of it: a single "RYLI Overlay" browser source. Hover (or tap) the picture itself, or the list beside it — either one highlights what each piece does.

That one overlay does two jobs. It automatically runs the engagement graphics — leaderboard, milestone celebrations, the hype meter — the moment it's added, no extra setup. And if you want, it can also show your own face: piped straight from your PC's webcam through RYLI, not a second OBS source. It's off by default, and when it's on, it fills that same jumbotron box edge-to-edge — rotating in during quiet moments, or pinned on with a hotkey while you're talking. More on turning it on in the next step.

✨Step 4 of 5 — Install & onboard RYLI

Layer RYLI on top

With OBS and DroidCam already working, RYLI layers on top in a few minutes — its setup wizard walks you through connecting to OBS.

Download RYLI
Grab the free installer — same one used everywhere else on this site.
Run the onboarding wizard
RYLI opens straight into a short wizard. When you reach the OBS step, make sure OBS is open with Tools → WebSocket Server Settings → Enable WebSocket server checked, then tap Connect & Add Overlay — it adds the "RYLI Overlay" browser source for you, no manual scene-building.
Turn on your face cam (optional)
In Settings → Configure Ryli → Webcam, check "Rotate my camera through the jumbotron" and pick your device. It's free on every plan, and off by default. Once it's on, hotkeys let you fine-tune it live (hold to pin it full-time, release to let it rotate again, disable to turn it off) — good to know, not required to get started.
Pick a theme
Choose any of the 3 free themes to match your brand. Next up — actually starting your show.
🟢Step 5 of 5 — Start your Whatnot show

Go live the right way

Whatnot is RYLI's launch platform today, with more platforms planned. Following this exact sequence matters — it's what lets RYLI's data capture and effects trigger correctly, not just "some window with Whatnot open."

Click "🎥 Go Live" in RYLI
This opens a dedicated window just for Whatnot — RYLI watches everything that happens inside it.
Log into Whatnot in that window
Same as you always do. This window stays separate from your regular browser.
On Whatnot's site: Shows → OBS Tools → Connect to OBS
These are Whatnot's own buttons, on their website — Connect to OBS sits in the bottom-right of the page.
Start Show → Open Show
Also Whatnot's own buttons. This is what actually puts you live and lets RYLI start capturing your show correctly.
Leave that window running
Run your whole auction from this same window, or leave it open in the background and control the auction from a second phone or tablet logged into the same Whatnot account. Either way — don't close this window mid-show.
🍎On a Mac — running RYLI in Parallels

Yes, it works on a Mac

RYLI is a Windows app. On a Mac it runs inside Parallels Desktop, and hosts are already streaming that way. RYLI recognises when it's running in Parallels and sets itself up accordingly — you press the same button everyone else presses, and there is no address to look up.

Keep OBS on the Mac side
This is the setup to aim for. OBS runs natively on macOS where it can use your Mac's own video encoder, and RYLI runs in the Windows window beside it. RYLI finds OBS across that boundary by itself.
Press "Connect & Add Overlay to OBS" exactly as normal
Settings → Connections. RYLI checks the Windows side first, then the Mac hosting it, connects to whichever is running OBS, and points OBS back at an address that can actually reach it. Windows may ask whether to allow RYLI through the firewall the first time — choose Allow, or OBS won't be able to load your overlay.
Set your own OBS password instead of copying one
Parallels' shared clipboard can be slow, and OBS generates a long random password. In OBS go to Tools → WebSocket Server Settings, type a short password of your own into the password box, and Apply. Then type that same one into RYLI. No copy-paste involved, so nothing can lag or paste half of it.
If RYLI can't find OBS
Check OBS is open with Enable WebSocket server ticked, then press the button again. Still nothing? In Parallels open Settings → Hardware → Network, switch from Shared to Bridged, restart the Windows VM and try once more.
You can also run OBS inside Windows
That works too, with no special setup. The trade-off is that OBS can't reach your Mac's hardware encoder from inside the VM, so it falls back to encoding on the processor — fine on some machines, heavy on others. Try the Mac-side setup first.

A native macOS build isn't available yet. If you're running RYLI on a Mac we'd genuinely like to hear how it goes — email hello@ryli.app.
